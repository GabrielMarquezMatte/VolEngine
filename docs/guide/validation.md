# Validation against reference implementations

VolEngine's ARMA(m,n)-sGARCH(p,q) and its GARCH variants (eGARCH, gjrGARCH, apARCH, iGARCH, csGARCH and the
eight fGARCH submodels) with normal, Student-t (`std`) and GED (`ged`) innovations are validated against two
independent oracles: [`rugarch`](https://cran.r-project.org/package=rugarch) (R) for the default
conventions and [`arch`](https://pypi.org/project/arch/) (Python) for the `arch` options. The test is
`python/tests/test_oracles.py`; it reads committed fixtures, so it needs neither R nor `arch`. How the data and
fixtures are produced is described in [`tests/reference/README.md`](../../tests/reference/README.md).

ARMA is validated against `rugarch` only: `arch` has no MA term, and its AR mean drops the first m
observations from the likelihood instead of keeping them. Of the variants, `arch` has gjrGARCH (`GARCH` with
`o = p`), eGARCH and APARCH. Its APARCH backcast cannot be reproduced (see
[`arch`](#arch-ve_varinit_backcast)), so apARCH, like iGARCH, csGARCH and fGARCH, is validated against `rugarch`
only; their `VE_VARINIT_BACKCAST` recursions are the natural extension of the `arch` rule, without an oracle.

### Case selection

Each case runs on the series simulated with its variance orders (a variant, with its own model). Every oracle
estimate must be interior; the oracle scripts stop otherwise and the test checks it again. The rule is stated on
the weights, the non-negative entries that sum to the persistence P (α and β; β alone in eGARCH; ½α, β and
½(α + γ) in gjrGARCH; ακ and β in apARCH and fGARCH): every weight > 1e-4, P < 0.999 (not for iGARCH, where
P = 1), |η₁| and the apARCH |γ| < 0.999, and in csGARCH ρ − P > 1e-4 and φ > 1e-4.
Three choices follow from that rule or from an identification problem, all observed with `rugarch` 1.5-6:

- **sGARCH(2,2) needs its own series.** On every planned series (the three new ones and `t3000`) a (2,2)
  fit puts α₂, β₁ or β₂ on the bound (smallest weight between 7.4e-9 and 3.1e-6 for every distribution),
  because none of them was simulated with two lags of both kinds. Several sGARCH(2,2) parametrizations were tried; with α = (0.1, 0.15),
  β = (0.2, 0.5) and GED shocks every estimate is interior (smallest weight 0.097).
- **std and ged at (1,1) run on `sgarch21_std`.** On the normal plan-1 series `rugarch` puts the std shape
  at its upper bound, ν̂ = 100.
- **sGARCH(1,2)-std runs on `arma11_sgarch11_ged`, not on `sgarch12_norm`.** On the normal series the
  likelihood is flat in ν around 49 and neither oracle reaches its optimum: `rugarch` stops at ν = 48.72 and
  `arch` at ν = 50.10, with ℓ 6.9e-5 and 4.6e-5 below VolEngine's (ν = 48.34 against `rugarch`, 49.71
  against `arch`), and the shape then differs by 7.7e-3 (relative), above the parameter tolerance. That
  measures the oracles' stopping rules, not the likelihood. On `sgarch12_norm` the other (1,2) cases are
  fine, but on `sgarch21_std` and `sgarch22_ged` a (1,2) fit puts β₂ on the bound, so the case runs on
  `arma11_sgarch11_ged` (ν̂ = 10.6, β̂₂ = 0.32).

For the variants (`rugarch` 1.5-6, `arch` 8.0.0) every planned case is interior; four groups were moved because
they would measure something other than the likelihood:

- **std on a series with normal shocks moves to `egarch11_std`.** On `gjrgarch11_norm`, `igarch11_norm` and
  `fgarch_allgarch11_norm` the std fit drifts to the normal limit: `rugarch` stops at ν = 99.0 (its bound is
  100), 62.1 and 88.0, VolEngine elsewhere (gjrGARCH ν̂ ≈ 7e7), and the parameters then differ by up to 7e7
  (relative, the shape). Like the plan-1 std cases, that measures the stopping rules on a flat likelihood. On
  `egarch11_std` (ν̂ = 5.0–6.3) the three cases agree within the tolerances (so does `csgarch11_std`).
- **apARCH moves to `arma11_sgarch11_ged`.** On `aparch11_ged` the likelihood falls monotonically in δ from 0.6
  to 2 (`rugarch` with δ fixed: ℓ = −2903.7 at 0.6, −2908.3 at 1.3, −2916.1 at 2), and δ̂ = 0.32–0.49 (norm, std,
  ged). With δ < 1 the news term (|ε| − γε)^δ has an infinite slope at ε = 0, so the likelihood has a cusp in μ
  at every observation: `rugarch`'s Hessian SE of μ is 3e-5 (0.012 elsewhere) and VolEngine's line search fails
  at a point 0.02–0.71 below `rugarch`'s ℓ. The likelihood itself agrees: at `rugarch`'s estimates VolEngine's ℓ
  equals `rugarch`'s to 1e-13 on all three. On `arma11_sgarch11_ged` (δ̂ = 1.27–1.34) the three cases agree within
  the tolerances. `aparch11_ged` stays in the data, without a case.
- **csGARCH-norm moves to `gjrgarch11_norm`.** On `csgarch11_std` VolEngine's estimate (ρ = 0.9931, φ = 0.023)
  has ℓ 0.52 above `rugarch`'s (ρ = 0.9981, φ = 0.036) and satisfies `rugarch`'s constraints (ρ > P, φ ≤ β); at
  `rugarch`'s estimate the two likelihoods agree to 1e-13, so `rugarch`'s solver stopped short. On
  `gjrgarch11_norm` (φ̂ = 0.009) they agree.
- **The `arch` APARCH cases are left out**: its backcast is not the "Pré-amostral" rule (see
  [`arch`](#arch-ve_varinit_backcast)).

## Conventions

The oracle sources were read to establish the conventions below; no oracle code is copied or translated.
Where the C source of `rugarch` is not installed, the convention was established from its R code and by
filtering fixed parameters with `ugarchfilter` and comparing the residuals and σₜ, observation by
observation, with candidate formulas.

### `rugarch` (`VE_VARINIT_SAMPLE`, the default)

Read: `R/rugarch-sgarch.R` (`.sgarchLLH`), `R/rugarch-helperfn.R` (`.checkrec`, `.makefitmodel`),
`R/rugarch-tests.R` (`.information.test`), `R/rugarch-numderiv.R` (`robustvcv`, `neweywestcv`),
`src/garchmodels.c` (`sgarchfilterC`) and `src/filters.c` (`sgarchfilter`); for the grid, `.sgarchLLH`,
`.sgarchfit`, `.makefitmodel` and `.arfimaxfilter` as printed from the installed package.

- **ARMA residuals.** For the first m observations (m = AR order) the residual is the centered return,
  εₜ = rₜ − μ, with no AR and no MA term. From t = m on,
  εₜ = (rₜ − μ) − Σᵢ φᵢ (rₜ₋ᵢ − μ) − Σⱼ θⱼ εₜ₋ⱼ, where an MA lag before t = 0 is zero. Checked with ARMA
  orders (1,0), (0,1), (2,0), (0,2), (1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2), (2,3), (3,0), (3,2) and (0,3) and GARCH
  orders up to 4: residuals agree to 4e-16.
- **Initial variance.** With the default `rec.init = "all"`, every likelihood evaluation sets
  m̄ = mean(ε²) over the whole sample, with ε the ARMA residuals at the current mean parameters, and holds
  σ²ₜ = m̄ for t < max(m, n, p, q): the ARMA orders count. From t = max(m, n, p, q) the recursion
  σ²ₜ = ω + Σᵢ αᵢ ε²ₜ₋ᵢ + Σⱼ βⱼ σ²ₜ₋ⱼ uses only in-sample lags. For sGARCH(1,1) without ARMA this is σ²₀ = m̄
  and σ²₁ = ω + α ε²₀ + β σ²₀.
- **Every term enters.** The log-likelihood sums all T observations, including the held ones.
- **Densities.** std and ged are standardized to unit variance, with the densities of `src/dist/densities.hpp`;
  ℓ agrees to 3e-10 (relative) on every std and ged case.
- **Criteria.** Per observation, with n = T and k estimated parameters: AIC = (−2ℓ + 2k)/n,
  BIC = (−2ℓ + k log n)/n, SIC = −2ℓ/n + log((n + 2k)/n), HQIC = (−2ℓ + 2k log log n)/n.
- **Hessian standard errors.** Square roots of the diagonal of the inverse of a numerical (Richardson)
  Hessian of −ℓ.
- **Robust standard errors.** `rugarch`'s `robust.se.coef` is a Newey-West (Bartlett kernel) sandwich with
  ⌊1.2 T^(1/3)⌋ lags over centered numerical scores. VolEngine's robust standard error is the QML sandwich
  H⁻¹ (SᵀS) H⁻¹ without lags, which is also what `arch` reports, so the two estimators differ by
  construction (up to 22% on these cases). The `rugarch` fixture therefore also stores `qml_robust_se`,
  the QML sandwich built in R from `rugarch`'s own numerical Hessian (`fit@fit$hessian`) and per-observation
  scores (`fit@fit$scores`), and the test compares VolEngine against it. The Newey-West values stay in the
  fixture under `robust_se`.

**Core change.** VolEngine started from the hypothesis that a lag before t = 0 is zero in the ARMA
recursion (so the AR terms entered partially for t < m) and that σ²ₜ is held only for t < max(p, q). Both
differ from `rugarch` as soon as an ARMA order exceeds the GARCH orders. On ARMA(2,0)-sGARCH(1,1)-norm the
old convention left ℓ 0.51 below `rugarch`'s, ar2 4.2e-3 and σₜ 4.7e-2 (relative) away; on
ARMA(1,1)-sGARCH(1,1), where m = n = p = q = 1, both conventions coincide. `src/mean/arma.hpp` now omits the
ARMA terms for t < m and `src/model/likelihood.cpp` holds σ²ₜ for t < max(m, n, p, q). Neither boundary
depends on a parameter, so the gradient remains exact; the unit tests compare it with central differences.
The same ARMA residuals are used with `VE_VARINIT_BACKCAST`, which `arch` does not validate.

### `rugarch`, the GARCH variants

Read: `.egarchLLH`, `.gjrgarchLLH`, `.aparchLLH`, `.igarchLLH`, `.csgarchLLH`, `.csgarchcon`, `.fgarchLLH` and
`.fgarchModel`, printed from the installed package. Its C filters are not installed: what they do was established
with `ugarchfilter` at fixed parameters, comparing σₜ step by step with candidate formulas.

- **Pre-sample (`sample`)**, for t < held = max(m, n, p, q):
  - eGARCH: log σ²ₜ = log m̄; gjrGARCH and iGARCH: σ²ₜ = m̄. Both as hypothesized.
  - apARCH and fGARCH: σₜ^λ = mean(|ε|^λ) over the whole sample, not m̄^{λ/2}: `mvar` is mean(|res|^δ)^{1/δ}
    (apARCH) or mean(|res|^λ)^{1/λ} (fGARCH). Confirmed to 1.6e-15 in σ on apARCH.
  - csGARCH: qₜ = ω/(1 − ρ) and σ²ₜ = m̄ + ω/(1 − ρ), not qₜ = σ²ₜ = m̄: the permanent component starts at its
    unconditional value and the transitory one, σ² − q, at m̄. Confirmed exactly on csGARCH(1,1), (2,2) and
    ARMA(3,0)-csGARCH(1,1) (q₀ = 2 = ω/(1 − ρ), σ²₀ = m̄ + 2, the same values for every t < held).
- **E|z| in eGARCH** is that of the spec's distribution (`egarchKappa`), as hypothesized; ℓ agrees to 2.5e-13
  (relative) on the three eGARCH(1,1) cases.
- **fGARCH power.** The shock power is δ + fk·λ, with δ fixed at 0 and fk = 1 in the free-power submodels
  (NGARCH, APARCH, ALLGARCH) and δ = λ fixed in the others: δ ≡ λ, as hypothesized, and `coef()` has `lambda`
  only. Names: `eta1i` (TGARCH, AVGARCH, APARCH, GJRGARCH, ALLGARCH), `eta2i` (AVGARCH, NAGARCH, ALLGARCH),
  `lambda` (NGARCH, APARCH, ALLGARCH); apARCH has `gamma1..` and `delta`. All match VolEngine's.
- **fGARCH smooths |z − η₂|.** In every fGARCH submodel, GARCH included, `rugarch` takes |u|, u = z − η₂, as
  √(u² + 1e-6). Inverting one filter step at u = −1.31, −0.36, −1.73, −1.57 (ALLGARCH) and in GARCH, GJRGARCH,
  NAGARCH, NGARCH and TGARCH gives 1.000000e-6 each time, and a numpy recursion with it reproduces `rugarch`'s
  fGARCH ℓ to 1e-11 on every submodel. apARCH uses the exact |ε|.
- **iGARCH.** `coef()` has `beta1` = 1 − α₁ (in general 1 − Σα − Σ_{j<q} βⱼ) with NA standard error; the
  Hessian and scores cover the estimated parameters only, and `infocriteria` counts only them. As in VolEngine.
- **csGARCH limits.** `.csgarchcon` requires ρ − (Σα + Σβ) ≥ 0 and Σβ − φ ≥ 0, VolEngine's P < ρ and φ ≤ Σβ.
  No case reaches them (smallest ρ − P 0.059, smallest Σβ − φ 0.80).

**Core changes.** Three conventions differed from the hypotheses:

1. apARCH and fGARCH under `VE_VARINIT_SAMPLE` hold σ^λ at mean(|ε|^λ) (`presample` in
   `src/model/likelihood.cpp`). Before, TGARCH ended 2.4 in ℓ away from `rugarch`'s optimum (σ₀ 29% off) and
   every case with λ ≠ 2 failed the tolerances.
2. csGARCH under `VE_VARINIT_SAMPLE` holds q at ω/(1 − ρ) and σ² at m̄ + ω/(1 − ρ) (`src/garch/csgarch.hpp`).
   Before, csGARCH-std ended 0.26 below `rugarch`'s ℓ, with σ₀ 32% off.
3. fGARCH takes |z − η₂| as √((z − η₂)² + 1e-6) (`abs_smoothing` in `src/garch/power_garch.hpp`; 0 for
   apARCH). Where the likelihood is smooth the exact form already agreed within the tolerances (TGARCH, APARCH,
   NGARCH within 4e-4), but at λ = 1 and λ < 2 it is not: on `fgarch_allgarch11_norm` the AVGARCH optimum sat on
   a kink (one zₜ within 9e-12 of η̂₂) and VolEngine ended `FAILED` with NaN standard errors, and ALLGARCH had one
   zₜ within 4e-7 of η̂₂, where |u|^{λ−2} left the Hessian indefinite and every SE NaN. Exact AVGARCH ended `FAILED`
   or with NaN SEs on all ten reference series. With the smoothing every AVGARCH and ALLGARCH fit on the ten
   series converges with finite SEs, and AVGARCH agrees with `rugarch` to 1.5e-5. An fGARCH submodel then equals
   its named model (fGARCH-GARCH and sGARCH, fGARCH-APARCH and apARCH, ...) only up to the smoothing: about 1e-6
   in σ² and 3e-7 in ℓ (relative). κ (persistence, reparametrization, the `backcast` pre-sample term) stays the
   exact moment.

No boundary depends on a parameter and every term is smooth where it is evaluated, so the gradient stays exact:
the gradient grid still matches Richardson differences to 1e-6.

**Optimizer stop rule.** A line search that finds no decrease at a point whose gradient is within √tol (the
threshold of the existing flat-decrease rule) now ends `CONVERGED` instead of `FAILED` (`src/optim/lbfgs.hpp`):
the search has reached the resolution of the likelihood. With the smoothing, TGARCH on the scaled series of the
scale-invariance test (`backcast`, ×100) ended `FAILED` at the optimum of the unscaled fit (ℓ and estimates equal
to 8 digits). No oracle case depends on it: the oracle suite passes 125/125 with the line-search-failure-to-`CONVERGED` rule
disabled, and only the C++ TGARCH scale-invariance test relies on it.

### `arch` (`VE_VARINIT_BACKCAST`)

Read: `arch/univariate/base.py` (`ARCHModel.fit`, `ARCHModel.compute_param_cov`, `ARCHModel._loglikelihood`),
`arch/univariate/volatility.py` (`VolatilityProcess.backcast`, `VolatilityProcess.variance_bounds`, `GARCH`),
`arch/univariate/distribution.py` (`StudentsT`, `GeneralizedError`) and `arch/univariate/recursions_python.py`
(`garch_recursion_python`).

- **Backcast.** b = Σ wᵢ ε²ᵢ over the first τ = min(75, T) residuals, wᵢ = 0.94ⁱ / Σⱼ 0.94ʲ. `arch`
  computes b **once**, from the residuals at its starting values (the sample mean for a constant mean, zero
  for a zero mean), and keeps it fixed during the optimization and in the covariance computation.
- **Initial variance.** Every ε² and σ² with a negative index is b, for any p and q: σ²₀ = ω + (Σα + Σβ) b.
  The t = 0 term enters the log-likelihood.
- **Densities.** `t` and `ged` are standardized to unit variance and parametrized by `nu`, VolEngine's
  `shape`; ℓ agrees to 1.3e-10 (relative) on every std and ged case.
- **Criteria.** Totals: AIC = −2ℓ + 2k, BIC = −2ℓ + k log n. `arch` reports no HQIC or SIC.
- **Hessian standard errors.** Inverse of a numerical Hessian of −ℓ.
- **Robust standard errors.** H⁻¹ C H⁻¹ scaled to the sample, where C is the sample covariance of the
  numerical per-observation scores (centered, divisor n − 1). At the optimum the scores average to zero, so
  this equals VolEngine's H⁻¹ (SᵀS) H⁻¹ up to a factor √(n/(n − 1)) on the standard errors.
- **Variance bounds.** `arch` clamps σ²ₜ to loose bounds derived from an EWMA of ε²; they do not bind at
  these estimates (σₜ agrees with VolEngine to 3.0e-5).

The variants (read: `GARCH` with `o > 0`, `EGARCH` and `APARCH` in `arch/univariate/volatility.py`;
`garch_recursion_python`, `egarch_recursion_python` and `aparch_recursion_python`):

- **gjrGARCH** is `GARCH(p, o=p, q)`, with `gamma[i]` VolEngine's `gammai`. A lag before t = 0 reads b for ε²
  and σ² and b/2 for 1[ε < 0]ε², as hypothesized.
- **eGARCH.** `arch`'s ln σ²ₜ = ω + Σ αᵢ(|zₜ₋ᵢ| − √(2/π)) + Σ γᵢ zₜ₋ᵢ + Σ βⱼ ln σ²ₜ₋ⱼ centers |z| at √(2/π) for
  every distribution. `oracle_arch.py` maps `alpha[i]` → `gammai` and `gamma[i]` → `alphai`, and sets
  ω = ω_arch + Σ α_arch,ᵢ (E|z| − √(2/π)) with E|z| at the fitted shape; the standard error of that ω also depends
  on α and the shape, so it is compared in norm only (`null` otherwise). The backcast is ln b, and `arch` drops
  both shock terms of a lag before t = 0: in VolEngine's parametrization the |z| term there is γᵢ(√(2/π) − E|z|),
  not 0 as hypothesized; the two agree in norm only. With the hypothesis, eGARCH-std and -ged had ℓ 1.2e-3 and
  2.1e-3 away from `arch`'s and σ₀ 3.2e-3 off. **Core change**: under `VE_VARINIT_BACKCAST` a lag before t = 0
  reads |z| = √(2/π) for every distribution (`src/garch/egarch.hpp`); the three eGARCH cases now agree to 8.3e-5.
- **APARCH.** A lag before t = 0 reads σ^δ = b^{δ/2} and (|ε| − γε)^δ = b^{δ/2} (`shock = backcast**0.5`, without
  the asymmetry term), so the shock moment is taken as 1, where the "Pré-amostral" rule reads κ b^{δ/2}, the
  expected value of the term. Fitting `APARCH(p=1, o=1, q=1)` and VolEngine's apARCH with `backcast` gives σ₀
  1.6–3.0% apart and ℓ up to 0.13 apart on `aparch11_ged` and `arma11_sgarch11_ged`. The convention is not
  reproducible with the rule, so the `arch` APARCH cases are left out and VolEngine keeps the rule.

**Core change (plan 1).** VolEngine originally recomputed b from the residuals at the current μ and
differentiated it through μ. That is a different likelihood from `arch`'s, and on `t1500` it moved μ̂ by
1.4e-3 (relative), σₜ by 4.9e-3 and ℓ by 2.4e-3, failing the tolerances below. `VE_VARINIT_BACKCAST` now
centers the backcast on the sample mean of the series (or zero without a mean) and keeps it fixed, so b does
not depend on any parameter. The analytic gradient remains exact; the unit tests compare it with central
differences and check that σ²₀ does not depend on μ.

## Numerical settings

### `rugarch`: Hessian step (`hess.d = 1e-3`)

`rugarch` computes its Hessian with `numDeriv`'s Richardson extrapolation from a first step of
`hess.d` = 10% of each parameter by default. That step is too large where the likelihood is ill-conditioned
or not smooth, and the extrapolation does not remove the error. With the default step the worst differences
from VolEngine were:

| Case | Hessian SE | QML robust SE | Cause |
|------|------------|---------------|-------|
| `sgarch12_norm/sgarch12_norm` | 4.3e-2 (β₁) | 8.9e-2 (β₁) | β₁ and β₂ almost collinear |
| `sgarch12_norm/sgarch12_ged` | 4.4e-2 (β₂) | 9.0e-2 (β₁) | same |
| `sgarch21_std/sgarch11_ged` | 2.7e-2 (μ) | 5.4e-2 (μ) | ged with ν̂ = 1.33 < 2: \|z\|^ν is not twice differentiable at z = 0 |
| `arma11_sgarch11_ged/arma11_sgarch11_ged` | 6.0e-2 (ma1) | 1.2e-1 (ma1) | same, along the mean parameters |

Shrinking the first step makes `rugarch`'s Hessian converge to VolEngine's (central differences of the
exact gradient, relative step 1e-5): with `hess.d = 1e-2` every non-ged case agrees to 1.8e-4, and with
`hess.d = 1e-3` every case is within the tolerance (the worst, ma1 on the ARMA ged case, at 7.2e-3). The
`rugarch` fixture is therefore produced with `numderiv.control = list(hess.d = 1e-3)`. The setting changes
neither the estimates nor ℓ, only `matcoef`, `robust.matcoef` and `fit@fit$hessian`, and so
`qml_robust_se`. On the plan-1 series it also brings the Hessian SE agreement from 2.4e-3 to 1.4e-4.

### VolEngine: gradient tolerance (`tol = 1e-7`)

VolEngine stops when the largest gradient component of the mean negative log-likelihood, in the
unconstrained parametrization, is below `tol` = 1e-6 by default. On the ill-conditioned
`sgarch22_ged/sgarch22_ged` likelihood that stops early: ℓ is 1.1e-5 below `rugarch`'s and β̂₁ is 4.7e-3
(relative) away, above the parameter tolerance; every other case passes at the default. The comparisons use
`tol = 1e-7`, at which that case agrees to 1.2e-4 and ℓ is 9.7e-8 above `rugarch`'s. Tightening further
(1e-8, 1e-9) changes no estimate. The default is unchanged: the 4.7e-3 relative error in β̂₁ = 0.21 is
9.7e-4 in absolute terms, 0.5% of its standard error (0.20).

### VolEngine: Student-t near the normal limit

On data with normal innovations the std likelihood keeps rising as ν → ∞, and the fit stops wherever the
gradient falls below `tol`: on 44 simulated normal series (T = 5000) ν̂ ranged from 83 to 7e34, and on the
benchmark's TCC grid up to 1e211. Three computations are arranged so that this region stays smooth:

- **Log normalizer.** lgamma((ν+1)/2) − lgamma(ν/2) − ½ log(π(ν−2)) is computed, for ν ≥ 20, as
  a log1p(1/(2a)) − ½ + S(a + ½) − S(a) − ½ log1p(−1/a) − ½ log 2π, with a = ν/2 and S the Stirling series
  of lgamma (`student_log_constant` in `src/dist/densities.hpp`). Subtracting the two lgamma values, each
  about a log a, leaves an error of one unit in their last place: 1e-9 at ν = 1e6 and 1.5e-5 at ν = 8e9,
  more than the change of the mean log-likelihood along a step. The line search then saw noise instead of a
  slope, and 11 of 20 sGARCH(1,1)-std fits and 13 of 20 ARMA(1,1)-sGARCH(1,3)-std fits on normal data ended
  `FAILED` (one at ν = 1.5e15 with ℓ = +29704, a value made of that noise). The forward-mode derivative had
  the same cancellation.
- **Kernel.** ((ν+1)/2) log1p(z²/(ν−2)) replaces ((ν+1)/2) log(1 + z²/(ν−2)), whose rounding of 1 + z²/(ν−2)
  is multiplied by (ν+1)/2.
- **Quotients in `ad::Dual`.** The derivative of a/b is (a′ − q b′)/b with q = a/b, not a′/b − (q/b) b′:
  q/b alone underflows once |b| passes ~1e154, and the derivative of 1/(2a) in the normalizer was then zero.
  That gave a wrong gradient in ν past ν ≈ 1e154 and ended 2 of 360 std fits of the normal-innovation grid
  `FAILED`.

Below ν = 20 the normalizer is the lgamma difference, computed as before; elsewhere the old and new forms
agree to rounding (2e-12 at ν = 1e4), and every oracle case passes with unchanged tolerances.

ν is not bounded. For the standard errors, a std shape with 1/ν < 1e-8 (ν > 1e8) sits on the boundary of
the normal limit, as α and β entries below 1e-8 do: its curvature in ν falls as ν⁻⁴ and its cross terms as
ν⁻², below what central differences of the gradient resolve (at ν̂ = 2e22 that noise alone made −H
indefinite and every standard error NaN). It leaves H and S and gets NaN, and μ, ω, α and β come from the
remaining block. Below 1e8 the shape stays in H, with a standard error that grows as ν².

## Tolerances

Fixed in plan 1 before any comparison and unchanged for the grid and the variants; none was loosened. "Observed" is the worst
value over all cases of that oracle.

| Quantity | Tolerance | Why | Observed, `rugarch` | Observed, `arch` |
|----------|-----------|-----|---------------------|------------------|
| Log-likelihood, one-sided | ℓ_VE ≥ ℓ_oracle − 1e-6·\|ℓ_oracle\| | VolEngine must reach an optimum at least as good as the oracle's | ℓ_VE − ℓ_oracle ≥ −2.0e-8 | ≥ +1.6e-9 |
| Log-likelihood, two-sided | relative 1e-4 | Same likelihood, different optimizers and stopping rules | 7.4e-10 | 1.3e-10 |
| Parameters | relative 1e-3; absolute 1e-5 when \|θ\| < 1e-2 | Flat likelihood near the optimum: parameters agree less than ℓ | 3.7e-4 rel; ω 5.9e-6 abs | 1.2e-4 rel; ω 2.6e-7 abs |
| Hessian SE | relative 5e-2 | Numerical second derivatives on both sides with different step rules | 1.1e-2 | 1.8e-3 |
| Robust SE | relative 5e-2 | Same, plus the oracle's numerical scores | 2.1e-2 (QML sandwich) | 3.5e-3 |
| σₜ | maximum relative 1e-3 | Follows the parameter tolerance through the recursion | 9.6e-5 | 3.0e-5 |
| Criteria | absolute 1e-6 on the oracle's scale | Deterministic functions of ℓ, n and k | 1.3e-9 (per observation) | 7.6e-7 (totals) |

The shape parameter is held to the same tolerances as the others. Its worst differences are 5.2e-5
(estimate), 1.3e-4 (Hessian SE) and 1.9e-4 (QML robust SE) against `rugarch`, and 1.2e-4, 2.8e-4 and 5.8e-4
against `arch`.

On the totals scale the criteria tolerance is the tightest check: it holds only if ℓ agrees to 5e-7. The
worst case, `arma11_sgarch11_ged/sgarch12_std` (7.6e-7 in AIC), is one where VolEngine's ℓ is 3.8e-7
*above* `arch`'s.
